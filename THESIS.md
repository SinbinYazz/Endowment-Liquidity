# Value thesis: a commitment gate after a drawdown

**Customer and decision.** A university endowment investment office is preparing for an investment committee meeting after public markets fall. It must decide whether to approve a new private-fund commitment while meeting spending and capital calls. Public records show the underlying decision matters: the University of Kentucky committee reviewed an annual stress test comparing liquid assets with spending and call requirements; FIU Foundation's policy requires subcommittee approval for new private commitments when private NAV plus unfunded commitments exceeds 60% of portfolio NAV. Those are examples, not proof that other offices use the same gate or lack adequate tools. [1][2]

**Opportunity.** Give staff a fast, explainable, off-cycle view of a proposed commitment under changed market and cash-flow assumptions. The prototype shows a 12-month cash path, the new-commitment gate, and trade-offs from deferral or a hypothetical secondary sale. A client would validate the actual policy, data timing, and decision rights before product development.

**How big?** The FY2025 NACUBO-Commonfund survey included **657** educational institutions and related foundations, with a **$253.6M** median endowment. It is a survey sample, not a count of qualified buyers. [3] For a transparent *sample-based revenue scenario*, assume 25–40% of participants have this problem and would buy a focused module at $20–40k annually. Arithmetic: 657 × 25% × $20k ≈ **$3.3M** at the low end; 657 × 40% × $40k ≈ **$10.5M** at the high end. Both adoption share and price are unvalidated assumptions; this is neither a total addressable market estimate nor a sales forecast. The biggest uncertainty is whether offices would pay for a separate workflow rather than use their consultant or existing systems.

**Five client questions.**

1. What decision does your committee make from liquidity analysis, and when does an off-cycle review happen?
2. How current are private NAV, unfunded, call, distribution, and spending data when a decision is made?
3. What precise policy tests and approval exceptions apply to new commitments?
4. Which cash-flow assumptions and stress horizons does the committee accept, and who signs them off?
5. What evidence, audit trail, and comparison would make a changed pacing decision credible?

**Success metric.** Time to produce a committee-reviewable off-cycle scenario after inputs are available: proposed target **under 30 minutes**. Measure the current baseline with clients; no baseline is asserted here. A second quality check is whether a reviewer can reproduce each displayed number from the input and assumption ledger.

**Non-goals.** No prediction of markets or manager cash flows; no automated investment recommendation, trading, manager selection, portfolio optimization, statement ingestion, or real client data. The secondary-sale price is hypothetical and ignores execution constraints. The prototype does not determine whether spending can legally or practically be changed.
